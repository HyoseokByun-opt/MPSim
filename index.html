MPSim · Material Property Simulation

MPSim computes the effective properties of semiconductor materials from their microstructure. The microstructure is generated on a voxel RVE, the governing equations are solved on it, and the local fields of heat, current, stress and flow are visualised.

How it works

MPSim carries out the workflow of a digital-material study in one browser workspace. It runs on a single PC or on a server accessed from the network; data do not leave the machine.

1

Build the microstructure

Fillers of any shape, size distribution (normal, log-normal or a measured particle-size file) and orientation, pores, coatings, hollow particles, multi-filler blends and thin films, dispersed evenly or unevenly (a region, a gradient, clusters), on a voxel representative volume element (RVE) whose size is checked against measured criteria.

2

Solve the physics

Validated open-source solvers (periodic finite volumes, FFT-accelerated voxel finite elements, NASA PuMA, PoreSpy and openEMS) compute the effective tensors, which are compared with rigorous bounds.

3

Evaluate the fields

Interactive 3D and 2D views of every field, animated field lines, publication figures and a single-file HTML report; every number states the method that produced it.

Analyses

MPSim targets the materials of semiconductor packaging, devices and process equipment: moulding compounds, underfills, thermal interface materials, conductive adhesives, dielectrics, EMI absorbers, porous ceramics and filters.

Conduction

Thermal and electrical

  • Conductivity tensors, interfacial and contact resistance
  • Percolation of conductive fillers
Electromagnetics

Dielectric, magnetic, EMI

  • Dk, Df, field enhancement, permeability
  • Shielding effectiveness with a full-wave check
Mechanics

Elasticity, CTE and testing

  • Stiffness, engineering constants, CTE tensor
  • Thermal stress; α1 / α2 above Tg
  • Tension or compression test by displacement, force or pressure: force-displacement line, stress fields, load at the matrix strength
Rheology

Viscosity and flowability

  • Viscosity of uncured compounds, flow curves
  • Particle dynamics of the fillers: particle-size effect and shear thinning, on CPU or GPU
  • Maximum packing, underfill filling time, settling
Reliability

Moisture

  • Moisture diffusivity and saturated uptake
  • Uptake curve of a part, hygroscopic swelling (CME)
Porous media

Flow, filtration, acoustics

  • Permeability, filtration efficiency, tortuosity
  • Acoustic absorption, radiative extinction
Structure

Morphology

  • Porosity, size distributions, porosimetry
  • Pore networks, grain statistics, percolation paths
Inverse problems

Calibration

  • Unknown material values from measured properties, including viscosity
  • Credible intervals, identifiability, next measurement
Studies

DOE and optimisation

  • Factorial and Latin-hypercube studies, filler shares, one fixed grid for every case
  • Linked inputs: one factor sets several entries (x, 0.8·x, sqrt(x1·x2))
  • Realised filler content and shares reported for every case
  • Gaussian-process surrogates, Sobol indices, NSGA-II

Application example: a mould underfill from flow to cure

One compound is followed through the analyses relevant for a moulded underfill (MUF): epoxy filled with fused silica of 15 µm and 3 µm at 55 vol%. The viscosity of the uncured compound is computed by particle dynamics. The same particle dynamics shears a random packing in the resin, and the resulting arrangement is taken as the cured microstructure, on which heat conduction, thermal expansion, stiffness, permittivity and moisture uptake are solved (60 µm RVE, 240³ voxels). The presets MUF · epoxy + silica 15 + 3 µm 55 vol% and MUF · from flow to the cured part reproduce all results.

Viscosity of the uncured compound

The particle dynamics resolves lubrication between neighbouring particles, frictional contacts and van der Waals attraction; every particle translates and rotates in a periodic box sheared by Lees–Edwards boundaries, and the relative viscosity follows from the stress carried by the particles. The viscosity overshoots while the shear microstructure forms and reaches a steady value from a strain of about 6; it is averaged over the second half of the run: μr = 24 ± 3, the uncertainty being the expected scatter between independent packings. With the shear-thinning resin the compound reaches 33 Pa·s at 10 s⁻¹.

Microstructure after shear flow

The random packing is sheared with the flow along x and the velocity gradient along z to a whole-number strain (10), at which the sheared periodic images coincide with the ordinary ones. The number of close pairs (surfaces within 5 % of the sum of the radii) increases from 2.5 to 4.5 per sphere, and the pairs orient along the compression axis of the shear (135° from the flow direction) while the extension axis (45°) is depleted, as known for sheared non-Brownian suspensions (Morris, Rheol. Acta 48, 909 (2009)). The contents of the drawn structure equal the input (38.50 and 16.50 vol%).

Thermal conductivity

Steady heat conduction is solved with periodic finite volumes along x, y and z. The conductivity through the gap, 0.573 W/m·K, is 2.9 times that of the resin, 5 % above the Maxwell–Garnett estimate (the lower Hashin–Shtrikman bound, 0.544) and well below the Bruggeman estimate (0.685): the particles are separated by resin films, and the heat flux is highest in the silica and in the thin gaps between near-contacting particles. The changes caused by the shear (up to 1.6 %) are smaller than the statistical scatter of one realisation. Refining the grid from 240³ to 360³ changes k_z by +0.12 %.

Thermal expansion and stiffness

FANS solves six unit strains and a uniform temperature change. The volumetric mean CTE, 18.4 ppm/K, lies below the Kerner estimate for separated spheres (22.6 ppm/K), since near-contacting particles restrain the resin; the Hill modulus, 13.3 GPa (ν 0.26), lies within the Hashin–Shtrikman bounds (9.4–30.2 GPa). Fewer close pairs lie along the flow direction x, where E is lowest and α highest; the difference (up to 5 %) is comparable with the scatter of one realisation. For a temperature rise of 100 K with the RVE held at its size, the von Mises stress concentrates in the fine filler and at near contacts (mean 15 MPa, 99th percentile 68 MPa in the 3 µm silica).

Permittivity and dielectric loss

A quasi-static complex conduction solution gives Dk 3.845 in all three directions. The permittivities of silica (3.8) and epoxy (3.9) differ by 2.6 %, so that the Hashin–Shtrikman bounds coincide and the local field departs from the applied field by at most 2.9 %. The dissipation factor, 0.0068, equals the volume-weighted mean of the phases: fused silica leaves Dk unchanged and lowers Df in proportion to the resin it replaces.

Moisture uptake and swelling

Diffusion is solved on the water activity, which is continuous across the phase boundaries; the silica does not absorb water. The effective diffusivity is 0.67 times that of the resin, 15 % below the Maxwell estimate for impermeable spheres (0.78), because the diffusion paths wind between closely spaced particles. The silica restrains the hygroscopic swelling to 0.23 % at saturation.

The workspace

The ribbon follows the order of a study. The command panel on the left holds the inputs, the centre shows the 3D view, the Result Viewer or the report, and the inspector on the right controls the display.

Verification

The engine is verified against problems with known solutions. Every run also reports its own checks (RVE size, resolution, particle count, convergence and rigorous bounds) as passed, warning or failed, together with the reason.

Download and install

PC without internet

Download MPSim-…-offline-win64.zip from the latest release. It contains the official Python runtime, every wheel including NASA PuMA, and openEMS. Unzip to a short path such as C:\MPSim, run 1_INSTALL_OFFLINE.bat, then 2_RUN_LOCAL.bat.

PC with internet and conda

git clone https://github.com/HyoseokByun-opt/MPSim.git C:\MPSim
cd C:\MPSim
1_INSTALL.bat
2_RUN_LOCAL.bat
