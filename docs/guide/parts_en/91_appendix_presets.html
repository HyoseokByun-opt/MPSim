Appendix B. Presets

Presets are complete set-ups ready to run: materials, shapes, contents, resolution and analyses are all set; choose one and run.

Use them in two ways:

  • When learning — run a similar preset, look at the results, then change the values little by little.
  • When looking for a modelling technique — answers to questions such as "how do I lay platelets flat?", "hollow particles?", "interface resistance?" are in the Technique column below. Loading that preset and inspecting its settings is the fastest way.

B.1 Semiconductor package materials

PresetWhat it modelsTechnique to learn
EMC · epoxy + fused silica 50 vol% Semiconductor moulding compound; spherical silica dominates the CTE and conductivity. Giving a log-normal size distribution (cv 0.25)
Underfill · epoxy + SiO₂ 40 vol% (1 µm) Flip-chip underfill; the goal is CTE matching with the solder bumps. Basic set-up of a fine monodisperse filler
Low-Dk · PPE + hollow silica 30 vol% A substrate with Dk lowered by hollow silica. Setting up hollow particles (shell); the 0.3 µm shell thickness decides the resolution needed.
High-k · epoxy + BaTiO₃ 30 vol% Dielectric for embedded capacitors. Seeing how the distance between particles dominates Dk for high-contrast dielectric fillers
Low CTE · epoxy + ZrW₂O₈ 40 vol% A composite with the CTE lowered by a negative-expansion filler. Handling a negative CTE
Sintered Ag · 15 % porosity Die attach for power modules: how much the pores cut thermal and electrical conduction and stiffness. Adding network-shaped pores
ECA · epoxy + Ag flakes 20 vol% Electrically conductive adhesive; compares in-plane and through-thickness percolation. Laying flat platelets (spheroid, aspect ratio 0.15) and allowing overlap

B.3 Electromagnetics

PresetWhat it modelsTechnique to learn
EMI · epoxy + Ni rods 12 vol% Magnetic metal rods; electrical percolation and permeability decide the shielding together. Forming a network at low content with rods (spherocylinders). Compare percolation at only 12 vol% with spheres (example C of chapter 8 fails even at 30 vol%).

B.4 Porous media and filters

PresetWhat it modelsTechnique to learn
Porous low-k · SiO₂ + 30 % open pores Mesoporous silica low-k film, including the rarefied gas in nanopores. Switching on the Knudsen correction (chapter 17), essential with 5 nm pores.
Porous Al₂O₃ · 35 % open pores Porous ceramic for vacuum chucks and filters, of the same family as example B of this guide. A set-up running permeability, tortuosity, intrusion, pore network, radiation and percolation at once
PTFE membrane · 70 % porosity Filtration membrane for ultrapure water: largest through-pore and isopropanol bubble point. Changing the intrusion fluid; values are comparable only with the liquid used in the experiment (chapter 22).
Sintered SUS316L · 40 % porosity Point-of-use gas filter: permeability and pore entry size of sintered stainless steel. Handling metallic porous bodies; the pressure loss is converted for a 2 mm thickness.

B.5 Insulation and acoustics

PresetWhat it modelsTechnique to learn
Glass-fibre insulation · 8 vol% Equipment insulation that also absorbs sound; fibres lie in the xy plane. A structure whose matrix is air (the filler is the solid): fibre shape (cylinder), absorption at 25 mm thickness, radiation at 600 K (chapters 18, 19).

B.6 Choosing a preset

What you wantPreset to start from
Remove heat with fillersTIM · epoxy + AlN; for interface resistance, TIM · silicone + Al₂O₃
Lower the CTEUnderfill; lower still, Low CTE · ZrW₂O₈
Make it conduct electricityECA · Ag flakes (platelets), EMI · Ni rods (rods)
Lower or raise the permittivityLow-Dk · hollow silica / High-k · BaTiO₃
Block electromagnetic wavesEMI · Ni rods
Pass or filter fluidsPorous Al₂O₃, PTFE membrane, Sintered SUS316L
Insulate or absorb soundGlass-fibre insulation
Direction-dependent propertiesh-BN platelets or ECA · Ag flakes (orientation settings)
After loading a preset anything can be changed. Replace the materials with the properties of the material actually used, match the content and particle size, and it becomes a model of that material. Always check the Verification afterwards — the resolution of the preset may be insufficient for the new conditions (chapter 31).
