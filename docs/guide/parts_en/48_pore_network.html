23. Pore network

Open-source code: PoreSpy SNOW2 algorithm — divides the pore space into pores and throats

Simplifies a complex pore space into a network of "pores" (chambers) and the "throats" connecting them. Its purpose is to show the connectivity that the pore-size distribution of chapter 22 cannot.

This analysis does not need OpenPNM. The network is extracted by PoreSpy's SNOW2 alone, so the feature works even when OpenPNM is missing from the offline package.

23.1 What the analysis does

A pore space is usually wide chambers linked by narrow throats. As a graph it tells:

  • how many chambers there are and with how many neighbours each connects — connectivity;
  • how much narrower the throats are than the chambers — the degree of bottlenecking;
  • how many dead ends and isolated chambers there are — useless pores.

The extracted network is the input of pore-network modelling (PNM). Phenomena too heavy for voxel solves — two-phase flow (water and air together), drying, capillary invasion — can be solved far faster on the network.

23.2 Relation to experiments

No experiment measures the pore network itself. It is an interpretation, not an observation. Each element has a corresponding measurement, though.

ElementCorresponding measurement
Throat sizeWhat mercury intrusion (chapter 22) and the bubble point (chapter 21) measure is exactly the throats.
Pore sizeEstimated by gas adsorption or X-ray CT image analysis.
Coordination numberHard to measure directly; usually obtained from CT images by the same method as this analysis.

23.3 How the simulator calculates it

  1. The distance transform of the pore space (distance to the wall) is computed.
  2. Local maxima of the distance are taken as pore centres.
  3. A watershed segmentation divides the pore space into pores.
  4. The narrowest cross-section between two touching pores is defined as a throat.
  5. A network is built with pores as nodes and throats as links.

23.5 Assumptions and limits

ItemDescription
Pores and throats do not exist as such A pore space has no boundary saying "the chamber ends here". They are partitions defined by the calculation, and their number changes with the algorithm settings. Do not treat them as absolute.
Over-segmentation One elongated or irregular pore may be split into several. SNOW2 is designed to reduce, not eliminate, this. Suspect an abnormally high pore count.
Voxel resolution Throats narrower than two or three voxels are not caught at all, so two connected pores may look disconnected — a common cause of low coordination numbers.
Pores on the boundary Pores cut by the RVE faces are recorded smaller than they are; the smaller the RVE, the larger the effect.
Geometry only The network itself carries no flow information. The actual permeability comes from the Stokes solve of chapter 15.

23.6 Reading the rendering — balls and sticks

Regions where many large balls connect are the main channels of flow and diffusion; a ball with a single stick is a dead-end pore. The lower the coordination number (sticks per pore), the more easily the flow is cut when a pore clogs. The network can be exported as input to larger-scale pore-network models (flow and adsorption simulations).
