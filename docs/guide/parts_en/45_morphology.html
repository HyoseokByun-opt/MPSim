20. Morphology

Open-source codes: scikit-image labelling and distance transform, NASA PuMA structure tensor

Before solving for properties, look at what kind of structure was actually made. Porosity, specific surface, particle count, orientation — the values on which the interpretation of every property result rests.

This item is fast to compute. Running it alone before the main analyses, to check that the structure came out as intended, is recommended. A content off the target or clustered particles show up here at once.

20.1 How it is measured

QuantityExperimental method
Porosity Archimedes' method (open pores from the weight change in water), gas pycnometry (total porosity from the true density in helium), and image analysis of sections.
Specific surface BET adsorption: a monolayer of nitrogen on the surface, its amount giving the area, usually in m²/g.
Particle size and count Laser-diffraction particle sizing, SEM image analysis, sedimentation.
Orientation A structure tensor from X-ray CT or SEM sections, or polarisation and X-ray diffraction.
BET values and computed values differ in unit and meaning. BET measures roughness down to molecular size (about 0.35 nm) and gives much larger values; the computed value is a geometric surface down to the voxel size. The same sample can differ by several to tens of times, so do not compare the two directly. Use the computed value for comparisons between conditions.

20.2 Reading the numbers

ItemMeaning and use
Phase volume fractions Volume actually taken by each phase — the first value to check. If it differs from the target, the generation did not finish (not all particles fitted): lower the content or allow overlap.
Open / closed porosity Pores connected to the outside and trapped pores. Permeability, diffusion and absorption use only open pores; conduction and elasticity feel both. If the two differ greatly, find out why first.
Specific surface area Interface area per unit volume (1/m), tied directly to thermal resistance, pressure loss and reaction rates; also shown per phase interface.
Interface area per phase pair How much each pair of phases touches — the reference quantity for interface thermal resistance.
ItemMeaning and use
Particle count Particles actually in the RVE — the basis of statistical representativeness. With only a few dozen, the results shift with the seed. Example A has 226, ample for a composite of spherical fillers.
Equivalent diameter Diameter of the sphere of equal volume — the value to compare with particle sizing.
Structure tensor / orientation In which direction the structure is stretched. Randomly placed spheres should give three similar eigenvalues. One large value means orientation along it, and the anisotropy of the properties should follow that direction.
Anisotropy index Close to 0 means isotropic. Compare it with the anisotropy of the property tensors to check that they do not contradict each other.

20.3 How to use these values

SituationWhat to check
A property is lower than expected First whether the actual content fell short of the target.
The permeability is 0 The open porosity: many pores may all be trapped.
The directions differ Whether the structure tensor supports that direction. If not, it is a statistical fluctuation of a small RVE.
Results shift with the seed The particle count. If small, enlarge the RVE.

20.4 Limits of the calculation

ItemDescription
The voxel resolution sets the surface A finer resolution keeps increasing the surface (finer staircase). Comparisons at the same resolution are meaningful, more than absolute values.
Staircase effect A voxelised sphere has more surface than a smooth one. The program smooths with marching cubes to reduce, not remove, this.
Particles crossing the boundary Continue through the opposite face in a periodic structure and are counted once.

20.5 Reading the renderings — local thickness

  • Red — wide pores (chambers) or large particles. Cyan and blue — narrow throats or thin parts.
  • In the pores, the size difference between chambers and throats is what causes the ink-bottle effect of mercury intrusion (chapter 22).
  • Within one particle the value should be almost constant. A value varying strongly within a particle means it has merged with a neighbour and is seen as one lump (contact agglomeration).
