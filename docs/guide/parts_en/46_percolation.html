21. Percolation and passing particle size

Open-source basis: scikit-image distance transform and shortest paths (MCP_Geometric) — own implementation

Answers "how large a particle can pass through this structure?" Used for the largest particle passing a filter, the cut-off of a membrane and the flowability of powders. The result is not only a number: the path the particle actually takes is shown in 3D.

21.1 How it is measured — bubble point

There is a clear counterpart in the laboratory: the bubble point test (ASTM F316).

  1. The pores of the specimen are fully wetted with a liquid (a special liquid of low surface tension).
  2. Gas pressure is slowly raised on one side.
  3. At some pressure the first bubble appears on the other side; the opening it came through is the largest through-pore.
d = 4 γ cos θ / Δp

Raising the pressure further and recording the flow gives the size distribution of the through-pores (capillary flow porometry). This analysis computes exactly that value.

Easily confused with mercury intrusion (chapter 22). Mercury intrusion measures the size distribution of pores that can be entered; the bubble point measures the narrowest throat of a path that goes all the way through. The former includes inner pores, the latter only through-paths, so the bubble-point value always comes out smaller.

21.2 How the simulator calculates it

It asks whether "a ball of radius r can roll through the pores to the other side".

  1. The distance transform is computed: at each point of the pores, the distance to the nearest solid wall. Where it is ≥ r, the centre of a ball of radius r can sit.
  2. Only the region with distance ≥ r is kept.
  3. Whether that region connects one face to the opposite face is checked.
  4. r is reduced from large values to find the largest r that passes.
  5. For each passing size, the shortest path is found and drawn in 3D.

21.3 Reading the numbers

ItemMeaning and use
Phase The phase examined. Both the pores and the filler are checked.
Connected (x / y / z) Whether the phase is connected in that direction. Pores with ○ mean zero permeability and diffusion; a filler with ○ conducts no current (chapter 8).
Spanning fraction Fraction belonging to the spanning cluster. Close to 1, almost everything is one connected body; small, much of it is isolated.
Largest cluster Size of the largest cluster; changes abruptly near the percolation threshold.
ItemMeaning and use
Sphere diameter The particle diameter tested. The top row is the largest size that passes, to be compared with a bubble-point test.
Passes Whether it passes; smaller sizes further down naturally pass.
Path length The actual distance the particle travelled.
Path tortuosity Actual distance / straight distance. Larger particles detour more because they cannot use narrow shortcuts — a trend visible in the table.

21.4 Seeing the path

Show the path in the table switches to the 3D view and plays a ball of that size moving along the actual path. The ball is drawn at its real diameter, so one sees which gaps it barely squeezes through.

The paths of very small particles are drawn larger than real for visibility; a note at the bottom of the view says so.

21.5 A judgement by example

In example B (45 % open pores) the median pore diameter of mercury intrusion was 2.46 µm, yet the largest passing sphere comes out smaller. No contradiction —

  • Mercury intrusion counts sizes including wide chambers.
  • The passing size is caught at the narrowest throat of the path.

In short, a wide room with a narrow door cannot be passed. That is why filtration performance must be judged by this value, not by the pore-size distribution.

21.6 Assumptions behind the calculation

AssumptionWhere reality may differ
Rigid spherical particles Deformable particles (cells, emulsion droplets, soft polymers) pass narrower throats.
The structure does not change No particles widening the structure as they push through, no clogging.
No surface tension or wetting Solved as a pure geometry problem; factors such as the contact angle of the bubble-point pressure–diameter conversion do not enter.
Voxel resolution The point needing most care. When the narrowest throat is only two or three voxels wide, passing or not becomes uncertain. Check that the value holds at a finer resolution (chapter 29).
RVE size A through-path is a search for the best route in the whole structure. In a small RVE a lucky open route may or may not exist, and the value scatters. Repeat with several seeds.

21.7 Reading the renderings — connectivity and the passing sphere

  • Class map — 3 (red): clusters spanning from the top face to the bottom; 2: dead-end clusters attached to one face only; 1 (cyan): isolated clusters touching no face. In example A, 96 % of the AlN belongs to spanning clusters — why particle chains do the work in heat conduction.
  • Passing sphere — the largest sphere that can cross the pores to the other side (example B: 2.1 µm diameter) and its path. Larger particles or molecules cannot pass this structure. The path length ÷ thickness is the geometric tortuosity.
