6. Resolution and RVE size (Domain)

Most of the confidence in a result is decided in this tab: how large a piece is calculated, and how finely it is divided. It also decides whether the piece is a bulk repeated in every direction or a film of a given thickness.

6.1 What a voxel is

The calculation divides the structure into small cubes (voxels) — the three-dimensional counterpart of the pixels of a photograph. Each voxel is one cell of the computational grid, so

  • the smaller the voxels, the more exact the shapes and the more precise the results;
  • but the more of them there are: halving the voxel size makes eight times as many, and the time and memory grow by more than that.

The aim is therefore "small enough, but no smaller than needed".

6.2 RVE length and voxel size — the grid follows

In practice two things are set: the edge length of the RVE and the voxel size. The grid follows from them and is not entered separately.

grid N = RVE edge length L ÷ voxel size h (rounded up to an even number)
ItemMeaning
RVE edge lengthThe real edge length of the piece calculated. Larger is more representative and heavier to solve. Units: nm · µm · mm · cm.
Voxel sizeThe edge length of one voxel. Smaller is more exact. Units as above (e.g. 300 nm = 0.3 µm).
Grid size (computed)The grid and the number of voxels that follow from the two. The grid is rounded up to an even number, so the actual voxel size L ÷ N can differ very slightly; it is shown as well.

The units are for display only; the input file always stores µm. A convergence study — varying the grid to see whether the result converges — is done with the Grid size variable of the DOE tab (chapter 28).

6.4 Why 7× for Standard and 10× for Accurate

There is a widely used definition of "a size that counts as an RVE". Kanit et al. (2003, Int. J. Solids Struct. 40, 3647) define the RVE by the error accepted. When n RVEs of the same size V are built with different arrangements and calculated, the results scatter, and from their standard deviation D(V) the 95 % error of the mean is

ε = 2·D(V) / (Z̄·√n) ,   D²(V) ∝ V−α

— and when the size is too small, not only does the result scatter, the mean itself is biased. The criterion is therefore not a number of "times" but the size at which scatter and bias fall within the accepted error. It was measured directly with this program's generator and solvers — monodisperse spheres, periodic boundaries, ten different arrangements per size (six for E), 8–10 voxels per diameter.

CaseL/d = 457 (Standard)10 (Accurate)
k, 30 vol%, contrast 150±0.30 %±0.22 %±0.12 %±0.14 %
k, 50 vol%, contrast 150±10.4 %±4.3 %±4.3 %±1.9 %
E, 30 vol%, contrast 127±0.82 %±0.88 %±0.92 %±0.38 %

The 95 % scatter of one realisation, 2D/mean. The bias was within the scatter from 5× on (E −0.9 % at 4×), and +3 % (k) and +22 % (E) at 2×. α is 1.04 / 1.35 / 1.27 — doubling the volume roughly halves the variance.

Three things follow.

  • Below 5× the mean itself is wrong. No number of repetitions removes a bias. This is why Fast (4×) is for trends.
  • Standard (7×) stays within ±1 % with a single arrangement at ordinary contents of about 30 vol%.
  • At high contents and large contrasts (50 vol%, contrast 150) arrangements still scatter by ±4 % at 7× — the gaps and contacts between particles decide the conduction paths. Accurate (10×, three realisations) reduces this to 2 × 1.9 % / √3 = ±1.1 %. In such cases the Domain tab suggests Accurate or 2–3 realisations.
The false anisotropy of a finite box shrinks for the same reason. For an isotropic structure, the spread of the three directional values, (max − min)/mean, was 4.3 % at 7× and 1.9 % at 10× at 50 vol%. A directional difference of this size in an isotropic material is a trace of the RVE size, not anisotropy.

6.5 Thin film — a structure of given thickness

When the thickness itself is a constraint, as in a very thin layer, a periodic RVE that repeats in every direction does not fit. Choosing Thin film in the Domain section keeps x and y periodic, but z becomes the real film thickness.

ItemWhat changes in a film
ParticlesEvery particle ends inside the film. None is cut by the top or bottom face or wraps to the other side (in x and y they wrap as usual). Particles of the size distribution larger than the film are made as thick as the film allows, and the rest of the phase grows slightly to keep the content.
Matrix skin at the facesBy default one voxel of matrix is kept at the top and bottom faces (Matrix skin at each face, in µm; 0 lets particles touch the faces). Through the thickness the faces are the electrode plates, and a particle touching a face touches the plate directly. That contact area is set by the voxel size (where a sphere touches a plane, the point is inflated to a voxel), so the result wavers with the resolution — in a 40 vol% film, 0.6 % of the face voxels in particles raised kzz by 25 %, and on a 1.5× finer grid the excess disappeared together with the contact. Real coatings often have a resin layer at the surface too.
Particles through the thicknessNo limit. A film holding only 2–3 particles is solved as it is — the thickness follows the input, whatever the RVE criteria. The check fails only when not a single particle fits. Below two layers a note says that the result is not a bulk value but the strongly anisotropic value of that film.
Lateral length (automatic)The RVE criteria (7×/10×) apply to the lateral directions only. A thinner film has less volume and scatters more (6.4), so the lateral length is enlarged until the film has the volume of the cube of the same criterion: L² T ≥ (7 d)³. The particle count and the scatter equal those of the cube, and so does the number of voxels. Example: d = 5 µm, film 20 µm → lateral 47 µm (35 µm for a cube).
Conduction and dielectric analysesIn-plane (x, y) the two faces of the film are insulated; through the thickness (z) electrode plates are placed on the top and bottom faces — the conditions of a measurement across the film. The self-test checks exact agreement with the series and parallel values of a two-layer film.
Elasticity, thermal expansion, flow, diffusionSolved as the same film stacked on itself through the thickness (periodic in z). The Domain tab says so.
Values per thicknessArea-specific thermal resistance, sheet resistance and capacitance use the film thickness (instead of the Layer thickness of the Simulation tab).
Default directionThe through-thickness property matters most in a film, so every analysis solves z first, and the 3D and 2D fields and arrows show the z direction.

6.6 What the panel tells

Recommended RVE

Tiles show the grid, voxel size, RVE (lateral) length and expected particle count of the current settings, each with the reason it was set so underneath — 7 × the largest particle of 'Al₂O₃' (7.94 µm), for example, means that the tightest size criterion was "seven times the largest particle". A line below gives the Estimated peak memory per solver.

Criteria before solving

The criteria are checked before anything is solved, with the numbers of passed, warning and failed checks; for each check that does not pass, the actual and the required values are listed. A warning here is best fixed before running.

Two common warnings:
• The particles are too large — the RVE holds only a few. Enlarge the RVE or reduce the particles.
• The voxels are too large — the thinnest part is drawn with only a few voxels. Reduce the voxel size.

6.7 Realisations

Particle arrangements are random, so the same conditions give slightly different results with a different arrangement. With Realisations at 2 or more, the analysis is repeated on different arrangements and the mean is shown with its 95 % confidence interval.

A wide interval means the RVE is small enough to be swayed by the arrangement (6.4): enlarge the RVE or add realisations. A fixed Random seed rebuilds the same arrangement, which helps when results must be reproduced; the structure of a seed is kept in the run and reused by the next analysis (2.4).

6.8 Automatic enlargement

With Enlarge the RVE when the criteria are not met on, the program enlarges the RVE automatically when the criteria are not met (only the lateral length for a film). Convenient, but the solving time can grow beyond expectation; when the time must be controlled, switch it off and adjust by hand.

6.9 Grid limit and memory

A limit on the grid prevents a mistyped voxel size from using up the whole memory of the PC. It is a safeguard, not a physical limit.

The default limit since v4 is 500 × 500 × 500 voxels for every analysis. Beyond that a workstation can hardly solve, and the memory each analysis needs is given by the estimate below.

AnalysisDefault limitLoad at 500³
Conduction (thermal, electrical, dielectric, magnetic)500³Solves one field at a time; the lightest.
Flow (permeability, acoustics)500³Stokes flow carries four fields per node and uses much memory.
Elasticity and thermal expansion500³Solves seven load cases and is the heaviest (chapter 12); tens of GB at this size.

When a grid beyond the limit would be needed, the voxel is enlarged first; if that is not enough the RVE is reduced, and the check list records it.

Removing the limit

On a powerful PC the limit may be unnecessary. Giving 0 when starting the server removes that limit completely.

python run_web.py --max-voxels 0 --max-voxels-elastic 0 --max-voxels-flow 0

Without a limit the Grid tile shows no grid limit, and the grid the accuracy level asks for is used as it is.

Then what is the limit? — memory

With the limit raised or removed, memory, not the grid, becomes the real constraint. The check list therefore shows the Estimated peak memory, counted from the arrays every solver allocates: this much free RAM is needed.

It is judged against the memory of that PC — more than half of the installed RAM is a warning, more than 90 % a failure, but nothing is refused. A 40 GB analysis fails on a 16 GB laptop and passes on a 512 GB workstation. The user knows the PC, so the program does not refuse but gives the real numbers and leaves the decision. It is an estimate, not a measurement; keep a margin.

When several analyses run at once, multiply by their number. Three 16 GB analyses in parallel need 48 GB. Independent solves side by side within one analysis use their own share each too, but there the program reduces how many run side by side to the memory free at the start. The number of parallel analyses and the cores per analysis are set in the Home tab (section 3.7).
