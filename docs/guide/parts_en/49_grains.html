24. Grain analysis

Open-source code: scikit-image labelling and distance transform — every particle counted individually

Counts the particles of the generated structure one by one and reports their size, shape and arrangement as statistics, together with the thickness of the matrix left between them. The most diagnostic item of the program: it explains why the property results came out as they did.

24.1 How it is measured

MethodDescription
SEM and optical sections The specimen is cut, polished and imaged; image analysis counts the particles. 3D sizes are estimated from 2D sections and need a correction (stereology). This calculation is 3D from the start and needs none.
Laser-diffraction sizing Measures the size distribution of the powder — the value before compounding, which may change by agglomeration or breakage during mixing.
X-ray CT Gives the 3D structure of the real part, processed in the same way as this analysis — the most direct comparison.
EBSD Measures grain size and orientation in metals and ceramics.

24.2 Reading the numbers — particle statistics

Item on screenExample AMeaning and use
Particles in the RVE226 Particles actually placed — the basis of statistical representativeness. With only a few dozen, the results shift with the seed; 226 is ample for spherical fillers.
Number density2.82e7 1/mm³Particles per unit volume.
D10 / D50 / D90 (number)3.002 / 3.002 / 3.002 µm Number-based size distribution. Three equal values mean perfectly monodisperse — as designed. Real powders are not; to match one, give a size distribution in the Structure tab.
D50 (volume)3.002 µm Volume-based median. Laser diffraction usually reports volume-based values, so compare with this one; for a wide distribution it exceeds the number-based value.
Sphericity (Wadell) / aspect ratio1 / 1 Perfect spheres. Cylinders or flat particles move away from 1, which connects to the anisotropy of the properties.
Nearest-neighbour distance (CV)3.435 µm (0.0005) Mean distance to the nearest neighbour's centre and its coefficient of variation. A CV of almost 0 means every particle sits at the same spacing from its neighbour — see the surface gap below.
Mean / minimum surface gap0.434 / 0.433 µm The gap from surface to surface. The default placement (Keep apart where the loading allows) aims at 2 voxels (0.8 µm), but at 40 vol% only about 1 voxel (0.43 µm) fits, so the particles were packed at that spacing (section 5.5). They are pushed against each other up to this gap, so minimum and mean are almost equal. This gap is the section that heat and current must cross.
Clark–Evans index
(1 = random, > 1 regular)		1.889 Whether the arrangement is random or regular. 1.889 is far more regular than random — the particles pushed each other apart to keep the gap. Real composites usually come out below 1 because of agglomeration; to imitate that, allow overlap.
Clusters / particles per cluster226 / 1 Number of touching groups and their mean size. 226 clusters of 1 each means not a single pair of particles touches.
Percolating x / y / z○ ○ ○ Whether the filler spans the RVE. Broken in all three directions — this one line is why no current flowed in chapter 8.
This one table explains several property results. In example A, clusters 226/1 and percolating ○○○ are why the electrical conductivity is at insulator level (chapter 8), and the minimum surface gap of 0.43 µm is why the thermal conductivity stays at 0.64 W/m·K despite 40 % filler (chapter 7). The first item to open when a property differs from expectation.

24.3 The matrix between particles — ligament thickness

As the screen puts it, "the width of the matrix films and bridges separating the particles". Why it matters is clear from heat conduction: heat must in the end cross these thin resin layers, and their thickness dominates the total thermal resistance.

ItemExample AMeaning
D10 / D50 / D901.357 / 2.124 / 2.780 µm Distribution of the matrix thickness. D10 (the thin end) decides the conduction paths — heat and current crowd into the thinnest places.
Mean2.189 µmMean thickness.
  • A distribution skewed to the left, with many thin sections, means well-opened conduction paths.
  • A long thick tail means wide resin regions without filler — a sign of poor dispersion or clustering that lowers performance.

Ligament field colours this thickness in the 3D view, showing where it is thick and where thin.

24.4 Limits of the calculation

ItemDescription
Touching particles count as one In overlapping or agglomerated structures several particles are recognised as one lump: fewer, larger particles. Not an error but a view of connected lumps, which the Clusters item reports.
Voxel resolution A particle of only a few voxels gets an inaccurate sphericity and size. Aim for sphere diameters of 8 voxels or more.
Particles on the boundary Joined across the opposite face in a periodic structure and counted once.
Statistics of the generated structure Measured on what was actually made, not on the design input. When the two differ, the generation fell short of the target — this table is what reveals it.

24.5 Reading the rendering — matrix thickness between particles

A map of the matrix thickness left between particles. Thin bridges (cyan, blue) are where heat and current cross most easily, and where dielectric breakdown and thermal-stress concentration (chapters 9 and 12) appear first. When comparing dispersion processes, the structure with more thin bridges at the same content is the one with more clustering.
