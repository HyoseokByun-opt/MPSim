28. DOE — running a range of conditions

Often "how much does it change when I change this" is more useful than the value of one run. A DOE (design of experiments) runs many times with systematically varied conditions and gathers the results on one screen.

In the laboratory this means making many specimens. In the calculation, you set the factors and levels and the rest is automatic. The 8 cases of example A took 1.7 minutes in all.

28.1 How to set one up

  1. Go to the DOE tab of the ribbon.
  2. Choose the factors — content, particle size, resolution, property values, flow rate: almost any input can be a factor.
  3. Set the levels of each factor, e.g. content 20 / 30 / 40 / 50 %.
  4. Choose the design. A full factorial runs every combination.
  5. Run it; the cases are computed in turn.

The Cases table

Factor values and responses side by side on one row. In example A, AlN content [%] and AlN diameter [µm] are the factors, Density [g/cm³] and Thermal conductivity [W/m·K] the responses.

a row opens that run in the Result Viewer

Clicking a row opens that case in the Result Viewer. When a trend looks odd, the structure and fields of that very case can be checked at once.

Scatter plot — the axes you choose

Fixed graphs cannot answer every question. Four selectors at the top decide what is drawn where.

SelectorWhat it takes
xAny input factor or response. Factor against response (trends), response against response (trade-offs) and factor against factor (how the design space is covered) are all possible.
y
colour A third quantity as point colour: blue low, grey middle, dark red high, with a colour scale at the right.
size A fourth quantity as point size: larger value, larger point.
Two axes plus colour and size show four quantities in one figure. This is how design-exploration tools present DOE results, and relations invisible when scanning the table often appear here.

Two particularly useful combinations:

  • Response against response — e.g. thermal conductivity across, density up: one sees at a glance "how much conduction must be given up to make it lighter". The boundary the points form is the trade-off front of chapter 29.
  • Factor against factor, colour a response — shows directly which corner of the design space is good, and makes unexplored regions stand out.

Only completed cases are drawn; running or failed ones do not appear.

Two things can be read from this table.

① The content is the dominant factor

From 20 % to 50 % the thermal conductivity goes from 0.42 to 1.13, about 2.7 times. Which factor to adjust first is clear.

② The effect of the size depends on the content — an interaction

At low content (20 %) the 1 µm particles give slightly higher values (0.4386 against 0.4173). At 40 %, however, 3 µm is clearly higher (0.8049 against 0.6795). The effect of the size reverses with the content.

Such a relation is an interaction, and it can never be found by changing one factor at a time. It is the main reason to use a DOE. In the graph it shows as two lines with different slopes drawing apart.

Physically it makes sense. At low content the particles are far apart and size hardly matters; at high content large particles come close to each other and form conduction paths more easily. Comparing the matrix ligament thickness of chapter 24 between cases confirms it.

28.4 A failed case is information too

Case 7 of example A (content 50 %, diameter 1 µm) failed. Not an error: the structure cannot be made under that condition. Filling small particles up to 50 vol% while keeping the minimum gap between them is geometrically impossible.

RemedyDescription
Allow overlapLet particles touch — the counterpart of agglomeration and sintering in real parts (chapter 5).
Reduce the minimum gapProvided the voxel resolution is sufficient.
Drop the conditionIf it cannot actually be manufactured, that fact is itself a result.

There are also failures not caused by the condition — the server was shut down midway, the user stopped the run, memory ran out. Then press Re-run N failed cases above the Cases table. Only the failed and stopped cases re-enter the queue with their original factor values; the results accumulate in the original case folder with -02 appended, and the study table switches to the new results. Successful cases are left alone.

Failed cases are excluded from the training data of the optimisation (chapter 29). Many gaps lower the accuracy of the surrogate model; with several failures, reconsider the ranges of the conditions.

28.5 Recommendations for designing a DOE

RecommendationWhy
Keep the resolution the same in every case With different resolutions one cannot tell whether a difference comes from the factor or the grid.
Coarse first, fine later Sweep the whole range at low accuracy to see the trends, then rerun only the region of interest precisely.
Two to four factors A full factorial grows with the product of the levels: 4 factors at 4 levels are 256 cases.
Consider the seed Structure generation is random. To see whether a factor's effect exceeds the seed-to-seed scatter, run the same condition a few times with different seeds.
Export to CSV For further analysis in other tools or merging with experimental data.
