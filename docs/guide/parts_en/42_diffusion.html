17. Diffusion and tortuosity

Open-source solver: NASA PuMA continuum tortuosity — the steady diffusion equation in the pores

How much more slowly a substance (ions, gases, moisture) passes through a porous structure. A key value in the design of battery electrodes, fuel-cell gas-diffusion layers, catalyst supports and moisture barriers.

17.1 What tortuosity is

Even through open pores a substance cannot go straight. It must detour and squeeze through narrow throats, so the apparent diffusion is slower than in free space. Two things cause the slowdown:

  • Porosity ε — the cross-section open to passage is smaller to begin with.
  • Tortuosity τ — within it the path winds.
Deff / Dbulk = ε / τ
Tortuosity is defined differently from source to source. Some call the τ of the formula above the tortuosity, some τ², and some use the ratio of path lengths (Lactual/Lstraight). This program shows the τ of the formula above (tortuosity factor). Match the definition before comparing with other data — the values differ by a square.

17.2 How it is measured

MethodDescription
Diffusion cell
(Wicke–Kallenbach)		 A tracer gas flows on one side of the specimen and a carrier gas on the other; the concentration that crosses over is measured. The steady flux gives Deff directly.
Time lag A concentration is suddenly applied on one side and the time until it appears on the other is measured; the lag is set by the diffusivity and the thickness.
Conductivity method
(formation factor)		 The most widely used. The specimen is filled with an electrolyte and its resistance measured. Ionic diffusion and conduction follow the same equation, so the resistance ratio F = σelectrolyte/σspecimen equals τ/ε — the standard method for battery electrodes.
NMR, tracer methods Observe the actual displacement of molecules directly.
The reason the conductivity method works is also the basis of this analysis — diffusion, electrical conduction and heat conduction are mathematically the same equation, so the same solver solves all three.

17.3 How the simulator calculates it

  1. Only the connected pores are kept; a substance cannot enter isolated pores.
  2. The steady diffusion (Laplace) equation is solved in them, with a concentration difference across two faces and periodic side faces.
  3. On the solid walls nothing passes (zero flux).
  4. The total flux gives Deff; the formula above gives τ back.
  5. This is repeated in three directions.

17.4 Reading the numbers

ItemMeaning and use
Tortuosity factor τ (x / y / z) Tortuosity per direction. 1 is a straight channel; larger means more detour. Example B's 2.078 is ordinary for a porous ceramic; above 5 the structure is very winding.
Open porosity ε Counts only the pores that actually carry diffusion; may be smaller than the total porosity.
Deff / Dbulk By how much diffusion is reduced against free space; equal to ε/τ. A dimensionless value to use directly in other calculations.
Formation factor F τ/ε — directly comparable with the conductivity method. If a measurement exists, compare it with this line.
MacMullin number How many times the resistance of the electrolyte-filled porous body exceeds that of the pure electrolyte — used in battery separator and electrode specifications; equal to F.

Very small pores — the Knudsen regime

Gas molecules normally spread by colliding with one another. When the pores are smaller than the mean free path (about 68 nm in air at room temperature and pressure), molecules hit the walls before each other. The diffusivity then becomes proportional to the pore size — Knudsen diffusion.

ItemMeaning
Knudsen number Kn Mean free path / pore size. Kn < 0.01: ordinary diffusion; Kn > 10: Knudsen diffusion; in between, the transition regime.
Knudsen diffusivity The diffusivity set by wall collisions alone.
Combined (Bosanquet) Both mechanisms together: 1/D = 1/Dmolecular + 1/DKnudsen. The slower one dominates.
This correction is a correlation, not a separate solve. It is computed from the pore size and the gas conditions; for nanopores read it as an indicator, and use a molecular-level analysis when a precise value is needed.

17.5 Assumptions behind the calculation

AssumptionWhere reality may differ
Pure diffusion No flow driven by a pressure difference (convection). When both occur, consider the permeability (chapter 15) separately.
No sticking to walls No adsorption or surface diffusion, which can make a large difference in real catalyst supports.
The solid blocks completely Diffusion through the solid phase (e.g. moisture in a polymer membrane) is not considered here.
Dilute solution, ideal gas Diffusivities that change with concentration are not handled.
Steady state The steady value, not transient diffusion.
Tortuosity is a property of the structure alone: once found, it applies to ions, gases or moisture alike — multiply the free-space diffusivity of the substance by ε/τ. That is the main reason to compute it.

17.6 Reading the rendering — how the concentration spreads

  • The concentration falls from top to bottom along the connected pores. Narrow throats where the colour changes abruptly are where diffusion is limited.
  • A pore that looks isolated in the section but has a single colour is connected outside the section; diffusion follows the 3D connections, not the section.
  • The effective diffusivity Deff/D₀ = 0.22 is smaller than the porosity (0.45); that ratio is the tortuosity factor τ = ε / (Deff/D₀) ≈ 2.1. It is the constriction of narrow throats, more than the winding length, that raises τ.
